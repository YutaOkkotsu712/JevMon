# Pipeline audit — 2026-09-24

This audit follows one decision end to end: `index.ts` wiring, `DecisionLoop`, the Jev payload (`features.ts`,
`instructions.ts`, `JevDecisionProvider`), the search (`search.ts`, `engineState.ts`), the blend, and the guards
(`dominance.ts`, `loopGuard.ts`). It was done at a ladder rating of about 1834–1863; the bot won 30 of its last 45
ladder games.

Each check ran against real data, not just code reading:
- 1,000 logged decisions from the last 40 games were rebuilt with the current code, at the live 38,000-byte budget.
- Every protocol message type seen in the logs was checked against what the tracker handles.
- Every field name in the instructions and glossary was checked against the rebuilt payloads.
- The places where the payload says "unavailable" or "unknown" were counted, with their causes.
- The search's engine state was inspected for logged positions.

## Open decisions (not changed)

1. **The search is counted twice in blend mode.** This is the largest wiring issue.
   - Every action in Jev's payload carries the search's `share` and `score`, and `SEARCH_INSTRUCTION` tells Jev to
     "treat a large share as strong evidence, and depart from it only for a concrete reason".
   - `DecisionLoop` then blends Jev's answer with the same search shares at `SEARCH_WEIGHT` 0.7. Jev is not an
     independent opinion, so the search's known biases get amplified (Substitute at +75, boosts at +30 a stage, heals).
   - Over the last 40 games, Jev's top pick matched the search's top on 53% of decisions. The final choice differed
     from Jev's top on 22%, and 179 of those 204 went to the search's top.
   - How much of that agreement comes from Jev seeing the numbers cannot be told from the logs. **Options:** leave
     `search` out of the payload in blend mode, so the blend combines two independent views; or keep it and lower
     the weight. Either is worth an A/B run over a batch of ladder games.
2. **`executionRisk` does not say which move.** It is attached whenever any modelled move knocks us out while we
   move second. It does not name the move or say whether it has been revealed, so a revealed Thunderbolt and a 56%
   unrevealed Explosion read the same. This is the gap behind the Beartic and Latias Tera turns; the guards are
   fixed, but the payload still flattens it. Naming the knockout moves, with their revealed flag, costs a few bytes.
3. **The search's `meanScore` is noisy.**
   - It averages each world's score with equal weight, so a world where an action drew a handful of visits counts
     as much as one where it drew thousands. The 0.03 override margin is applied to that noisy number.
     Visit-weighting would steady it.
   - `SEARCH_INSTRUCTION` also calls the score an "estimated chance of winning". It is the engine's heuristic
     evaluation, not a calibrated probability.

## Missing (lower priority)

- `-mustrecharge` is not tracked, so an opponent's lost turn after Hyper Beam or Giga Impact goes unseen. Rare in
  randbats.
- A type change (Protean, Libero, Soak, Burn Up) makes every estimate unavailable, because the `typechange` volatile
  is unmodelled. 1 decision in 40 games.
- Our Minior's speed is unknown in its Core form (3 decisions). Its stats do not fit the Meteor-form base stats used
  to recover the spread.
- If the provider hangs past the loop's own timeout while the search has also failed, the loop plays `actions[0]`,
  which is usually a switch. Jev's shorter internal timeout makes this very unlikely.

## Checked and sound

- **Guards:** all 24 exported guards are registered in `DecisionLoop` and run on every ranking. A skipped choice
  falls to the next-ranked action that no guard or cycle check rejects, and never to an invented one.
- **Wiring:** the loop's timeout is Jev's timeout plus 250 ms. The search gets its own budget. The override margin
  defaults to 0.03. A failed Jev call falls back to the search's ranking in blend mode.
- **Payload size:** 84% of live payloads are the reduced tier and 16% minimal. None was over the 38,000-byte budget.
- **Instructions:** every field named in the instructions and glossary exists in the code. Only four never appeared
  in 1,000 rebuilt payloads, and each needs a rare situation: `saltCure`, `itsSaltCureChipsThemEachTurnPercent`,
  `teraChangesOurForme` and `coveredProbabilityMass`.
- **Tracker coverage:** every state-changing message type in the logs is handled. The unhandled ones are cosmetic
  (`-supereffective`, `-resisted`, `-anim`, `raw`) or are read by `BattleEvidence`, which already discards damage
  followed by `-crit`, `-hitcount`, `-miss` and the like.
- **`endgame` and `loopGuard`:** behave as documented.
